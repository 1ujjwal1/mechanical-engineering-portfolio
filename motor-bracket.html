PROJECT 03 · BRACKET DESIGN & FEA

Motor Mounting
Bracket Design.

Designing a compact motor/gearbox bracket that fits the roll-cage envelope, supports defined service and impact-related loads, and allows practical assembly. The full vehicle assembly provides packaging and mass-distribution context.

SolidWorksAnsysTopology optimization planned

01. Design objective

Create a compact, lightweight bracket that supports the motor/gearbox under defined operating and impact-related loads while fitting the roll cage, allowing assembly access and preserving nearby clearances.

02. Constraints and initial approach

  • Mounting depends on roll-cage geometry and motor/gearbox mass.
  • A nominal 3 mm thickness is under consideration; it is not yet a validated final thickness.
  • The 232-component vehicle assembly, including roll cage and drivetrain hardware, supports packaging and assembly checks.
  • Topology optimization must preserve critical interfaces and attachment points.

03. Full assembly and mass representation

The full SolidWorks assembly includes the roll cage, drivetrain and driver to review vehicle packaging and the occupied envelope.

Vehicle calculations depend on total mass and CG location/height. Before physical assembly, CAD values remain estimates. Realistic densities, measured component masses and correct positions improve them, while physical weighing and CG measurement are needed for validation.

04. Supplemental braking load estimate

For a 25 kg motor stopping from 60 km/h to zero in 0.5 s, constant deceleration gives about 33.3 m/s² (3.4 g) and an equivalent inertial force of 833 N.

ASSUMED MOTOR MASS25 kg
ESTIMATED INERTIAL FORCE833 N
68 mm OFFSET MOMENT56.7 N·m

With a 68 mm offset perpendicular to the attachment plane, the moment is 833 N × 0.068 m ≈ 56.7 N·m. Direction depends on the coordinate system and free-body model.

Load-case distinction: this estimate is separate from the ANSYS screenshot with a 1,000 N global-X force. The 833 N and 56.7 N·m were not shown as solved results. This constant-deceleration estimate is not crash-impact validation; rerun the model with consistent loads and realistic mounts before design acceptance.

05. Structural analysis plan

  • Define realistic motor-mass, acceleration/braking, reaction-torque, chain and impact load cases.
  • Represent the load path through the bracket, fasteners/welds and roll cage.
  • Use realistic contacts and supports; do not fully fix interfaces that actually transfer load through fasteners or contact surfaces.
  • Review von Mises stress, deformation, fastener loads and stress concentrations.
  • Check mesh refinement and ensure unrealistic constraints or singularities do not dominate results.
One supplied safety-factor screenshot reports a minimum of 11.566 (display maximum 15). A separate 1,000 N global-X result set reports 3.0601, 81.696 MPa and 0.16674 mm. They remain separate because identical geometry, material and settings are not established. Neither is validated until loading, material, contacts, supports, mesh convergence and result settings are confirmed.

Material and bracket specification

The concept uses a nominal 3 mm thickness. Structural Steel is a provisional reference; the original model used custom material properties, so verify each ANSYS material card before treating results as final.

For reference only, typical ANSYS Structural Steel values are 200 GPa modulus, 0.30 Poisson’s ratio, 7,850 kg/m³ density and 250 MPa yield strength. Verify them against the actual material card and stock specification.

Using the assumed 250 MPa yield strength, 250 / 81.696 ≈ 3.06, matching the displayed minimum 3.0601 numerically. This check does not prove the solve used that material card.

Verify steel grade/certificate, service and impact loads, bolt/weld connections, mounting restraints, mesh convergence, and geometry consistency across plots.

07. Topology optimization

Topology optimization follows baseline validation. Preserve the motor interface, chassis mounts, fastener holes, minimum thickness and manufacturing constraints, then rebuild the result as CAD and reanalyse it.

08. Next steps

  • Confirm bracket material and final mounting details.
  • Document motor/gearbox mass and centre of gravity.
  • Quantify baseline bracket mass and target weight reduction.
  • Complete baseline FEA with realistic load cases and boundary conditions.
  • Run topology optimization and validate the reconstructed design.
  • Compare CAD mass/CG estimates with physical measurements after assembly.
