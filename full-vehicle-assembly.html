PROJECT 01 · VEHICLE-LEVEL INTEGRATION

Full Vehicle Assembly
& Mass Properties.

A 232-component SolidWorks assembly created to understand vehicle packaging and real-world assembly constraints, then estimate mass properties and centre-of-gravity (CG) location with the driver represented in the digital model.

01. Full vehicle CAD assembly

The model combines the roll cage, suspension, wheels and tyres, steering, drivetrain/gearbox, panels, fasteners and driver representation in one vehicle-level assembly. The aim was to test the overall arrangement before physical assembly, rather than evaluate each subsystem in isolation.

02. Packaging and assembly planning

The full assembly was used to understand where components fit, how the driver occupies the cockpit, and how the suspension and drivetrain interact with the frame. It provides a way to identify integration issues before building the physical vehicle.

CHECK 01

Spatial constraints

Review clearances around the cockpit, roll cage, suspension, steering and drivetrain.

CHECK 02

Assembly access

Consider fastener access, component insertion paths and potential service difficulties.

CHECK 03

Vehicle layout

Assess whether a shorter wheelbase is feasible without forcing wider redesign of the vehicle package.

The assembly is a planning aid. Fit, tool access, clearances and serviceability still need confirmation against manufacturing tolerances and the actual assembly sequence.

03. Mass-property assignment in SolidWorks

Selected components were weighed physically and their measured masses were assigned to the digital assembly. For other parts—including the roll cage, suspension arms, body sheets and panels—material properties or appropriate mass properties were assigned in SolidWorks. The driver was represented in the assembly so that the calculation better reflected the occupied vehicle configuration.

04. Longitudinal CG position and wheelbase decision

CAD measurement views were used to estimate the longitudinal distance from the CG reference to the front and rear wheel/axle reference points. These distances add to the selected wheelbase and help form a preliminary static load-distribution estimate.

CG TO FRONT AXLE REFERENCE90.36 cm
CG TO REAR AXLE REFERENCE51.88 cm
DERIVED WHEELBASE142.24 cm / 56.0 in

Using those two distances as longitudinal axle-centre references, the simple geometric estimate of static axle distribution is approximately 36.5% front and 63.5% rear. This is derived from the CG position and wheelbase; it is not a physical corner-weight or axle-scale measurement.

Why the wheelbase was retained

The wheelbase was treated as a packaging decision, not a dimension to shorten in isolation. Reducing it would require checking cockpit and subsystem packaging again and could change steering geometry/turning behaviour and longitudinal load transfer. A shorter wheelbase also increases longitudinal load transfer for the same mass, CG height and acceleration/deceleration. The selected layout was therefore retained as the current baseline rather than reducing wheelbase without a complete vehicle-level review.

05. Workflow and engineering application

  1. Assemble the modeled vehicle systems and driver representation in SolidWorks.
  2. Check packaging, subsystem clearances and potential assembly access issues.
  3. Assign measured masses to selected components and material/mass properties to the remaining parts.
  4. Use SolidWorks Mass Properties and geometric measurements to estimate mass and CG position.
  5. Use the estimates as preliminary inputs for vehicle-dynamics calculations, including static weight distribution, braking, acceleration and suspension studies.
  6. When the physical vehicle is available, weigh it and validate the CG and axle loads to update the digital model.

06. Outcome and validation status

The completed CAD assembly provides a vehicle-level package for review and a repeatable starting point for mass-property estimation. The current model reports 270.86 kg and a CG-height estimate of approximately 26 in; the longitudinal measurements support an approximately 142.24 cm wheelbase and a geometric front/rear static distribution estimate of 36.5% / 63.5% under the stated assumptions.

These are preliminary digital-model outputs. The total mass, centre of gravity and axle-load distribution should be checked against the final component configuration and physical measurements once the vehicle is assembled.
